The process

Five steps from first hello to finished stone.

No guesswork and no surprises. Here's exactly what happens once you get in touch.

  1. Get in touch

    Share your plans, a rough sketch or a few photos. We'll talk through materials and what suits the space.

  2. Your quote

    You receive an itemised price based on your chosen stone, edge profile and cut-outs.

  3. Digital template

    Once you're happy, we measure on site with digital templating for an exact fit to your cabinetry.

  4. Fabrication

    Slabs are cut and polished wet in our factory, then checked by the owner before they leave.

  5. Installation

    Our own installers fit, join and seal the stone, and leave the room clean and ready to use.

Common questions

Good to know before you start.

What do you need from me to prepare a quote?

A plan or sketch with rough measurements is enough to begin. It also helps to know the material you have in mind, the edge style, and how many sink, tap and cooktop cut-outs are needed.

When should the template be taken?

After your cabinetry is fixed in its final position and level. Having the sink, cooktop and tapware on site (or their spec sheets handy) lets us measure the cut-outs precisely.

Can I choose my own slab?

Yes. Natural stone varies from slab to slab, so we encourage you to see it in person. Visit the Southport showroom and we'll help you pick the piece and plan where the veining falls.

How do natural stone and porcelain differ?

Natural stone is quarried, so every piece is unique and most types benefit from sealing. Porcelain is manufactured, very consistent in pattern, and highly resistant to heat and staining. We'll match the material to how you use the space.

Will there be visible joins?

Long runs and L-shaped benches usually need a join. We plan their position with you beforehand, keep them tight and colour-matched, and place them where they're least noticeable.

How do I look after my benchtop?

Day to day, warm water with a mild detergent and a soft cloth is all it takes. Use a board for cutting and a trivet under hot pans, and wipe up spills such as wine, citrus and oil promptly.
